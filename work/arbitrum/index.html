Creator Program

An incentivized UGC campaign on X: turning ecosystem activity into creator-led distribution.

My role

Marcin PawelCampaign design & leadership

I designed and led Arbitrum’s Creator Program end to end, bringing together creator incentives, monthly editorial themes, ecosystem partners and original campaign creative.

The challenge

Less attention. Too little independent content. Declining network activity.

Entering 2025, Arbitrum needed to turn ecosystem strength into renewed attention and usage. Its dapps were the network’s user-acquisition hubs: tangible products people could discover, try and return to. Making those applications more visible was the opportunity.

Losing mindshare to Base

Base’s mindshare was roughly twice Arbitrum’s, making it harder for the network to command attention.

Too little independent content

80% of content came from Arbitrum’s official channels, leaving limited visibility beyond owned media.

Network activity was falling

Monthly transactions fell by about 20%, while TVL declined 25% between November 2024 and February 2025.

My response was to put dapps at the center of the growth strategy. By encouraging creators to explore and explain real products, I aimed to build distribution beyond Arbitrum’s own channels and give new audiences a practical reason to use the network.

Context: IOSG Ventures’ December 2024 analysis highlighted declining mindshare and competition from Base. Content-share and network figures are supplied campaign inputs; the approximate 2:1 mindshare comparison is based on the campaign estimate.

Campaign architecture

Connect creator attention with real ecosystem activity.

The growth hypothesis

Content about using Arbitrum apps could give audiences a practical reason to explore the network. Apps were the bridge between social attention and onchain activity: something concrete for creators to demonstrate, and audiences to try.

How the program worked

I designed the program around monthly themes across two seasons. Each theme gave creators a clear direction, while partner dapps provided real products and use cases to explore. A theme such as DeFi turned a broad brief into something practical: discover an application, understand what it offers and share the experience.

Creators brought those experiences to X in their own voice, producing explanations, walkthroughs and other content that helped audiences understand what they could do on Arbitrum. This connected the campaign’s editorial direction with the products behind the ecosystem.

Performance-based grants rewarded participation. The structure could then repeat with a new theme and fresh applications, keeping content varied while giving creators a consistent path from exploration to publishing and earning.

Ecosystem collaboration

I worked with 15 ecosystem partners, including USD.AI, gains.trade and GMX, to put real products at the center of each theme. Creators explored the apps and shared their experiences, giving audiences practical reasons to try Arbitrum.

What made it repeatable

Monthly themes gave creators a clear brief. Ecosystem partners supplied real use cases. Performance-based grants encouraged participation. Together, these elements created a repeatable campaign structure that could sustain fresh content while keeping it grounded in products.

Results

A stronger voice. A conversation beyond owned channels.

The communication challenge had two parts: regain attention against competing L2s and expand the conversation beyond Arbitrum’s official channels. The results show progress on both.

Arbitrum moved ahead of Base

In the supplied Kaito view for 2025, Arbitrum recorded 15.41% L2 mindshare, ahead of Base’s 12.22%—a lead of 3.19 percentage points.²

Independent voices became the majority

External content grew from 20% to 75% of all Arbitrum content on X. Official channels accounted for the remaining 25%, compared with 80% before the campaign.¹

+60%

Engagement on official content

Arbitrum’s official-content engagement grew 60% as external voices took a larger share of the conversation.¹

The L2 comparison and the 24-hour token snapshot cover different time windows and comparison groups. These observations show the change in visibility; they do not isolate the campaign’s contribution.

Ecosystem context

Track the ecosystem beyond X.

Social visibility was one part of the growth hypothesis. The intended next step was discovery and use of Arbitrum applications. During the campaign period, the supplied charts also show peaks in network transactions, liquidity and developer participation.

Monthly new developers rose from approximately 220 in January to 670 in October.

What this demonstrates

A connected growth hypothesis.

I built and led a program that brought 200+ incentivized creators and 15 ecosystem partners into Arbitrum’s marketing, giving real applications a bigger role in the network’s story.

The clearest change was who carried that story: external voices grew from 20% to 75% of Arbitrum content on X, while engagement on official content rose 60%. Arbitrum also finished ahead of Base in the supplied 2025 L2 mindshare comparison.

My contribution was to connect the campaign design, partner participation and creative execution around a practical goal: get more people talking about what they could do on Arbitrum.
